<p>If you have ever ordered bedding from an overseas site and received something that would not fit the mattress, you are not alone. Bedding sizes in Israel differ from American and European ones, and the gap is wide enough that the sheet simply will not close.</p>

<h2>Common mattress sizes in Israel</h2>

<p>Before buying bedding, you need to know exactly which mattress you have. These are the sizes you will find in most apartments:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Single:</strong> 90×190 cm</li>
<li><strong>Wide single:</strong> 120×190 cm</li>
<li><strong>Standard double:</strong> 140×190 cm</li>
<li><strong>Queen:</strong> 160×200 cm</li>
<li><strong>King:</strong> 180×200 cm</li>
</ul>

<p>The most confusing one is the standard double. 140×190 is an Israeli and European size; the closest American equivalent is a Full at 137×191 — close, but not identical, and different enough that an American sheet will either stretch or wrinkle.</p>

<h2>Why mattress height matters</h2>

<p>This is the part most people miss. A fitted sheet has to match not only the length and width but the height of the mattress. Older mattresses were 18 to 20 cm deep. Newer ones, especially foam or pocket-sprung, run 25 to 30 cm.</p>

<p>A sheet made for a 20 cm mattress will not stay on a 28 cm one. It will pull off the corners every night. Always check the stated pocket depth on the packaging.</p>

<h2>How to measure properly</h2>

<p>Measure the mattress itself, not the bed frame. Strip the existing bedding and measure three things: length, width and height — from the bottom seam to the top seam, without pressing down.</p>

<p>If the numbers do not land exactly on a standard size, round up. Slightly too big beats slightly too small.</p>

<h2>Duvets: one size up</h2>

<p>A rule of thumb that works: the duvet should be one size larger than the bed. On a 140 double, a 150×200 duvet gives real coverage for two people. A duvet exactly as wide as the bed ends in a nightly tug of war.</p>

<p>If the two of you sleep differently — one wraps up, the other throws it off — two single duvets on a double bed solves it. Common in Europe, less so in Israel, and it works well.</p>

<h2>Pillowcases</h2>

<p>The standard size in Israel is 50×70 cm. Pillows at 40×40 or 45×45 are decorative cushions rather than sleeping pillows, and their covers are sold separately.</p>

<h2>On materials, briefly</h2>

<p>In an Israeli summer, cotton wins almost every time. Polyester blends cost less but do not breathe, and they feel sticky on humid nights. If you are buying cotton, look for the weave: percale is cool and matte, sateen is smooth, shiny and warmer.</p>

<p>Thread count matters less than the marketing implies. Above 400 the difference is barely noticeable, while the price is.</p>

<h2>A checklist before you order</h2>

<ul>
<li>You measured the mattress itself, not the frame</li>
<li>You checked mattress height against the sheet's pocket depth</li>
<li>You chose a duvet one size above the bed</li>
<li>You confirmed the site lists sizes in centimetres, not inches</li>
</ul>

<p>Those four checks take ten minutes and prevent most returns.</p>
